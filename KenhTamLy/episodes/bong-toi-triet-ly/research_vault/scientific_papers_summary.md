# Scientific Research Summary - bong-toi-triet-ly

This document compiles peer-reviewed research papers and scientific frameworks for the episode "Bóng tối của Đạo".

---

## 1. Spiritual Bypassing & Avoidant Attachment (Buddhism Shadow)
- **Concept:** Using spiritual practices and beliefs (e.g., non-attachment, emptiness) to avoid confronting unresolved emotional wounds, psychological trauma, or developmental tasks.
- **Key Frameworks:**
  - **Coined by:** John Welwood (1984), who observed Buddhist practitioners using meditation to dissociate from emotional pain rather than integrating it.
  - **Spiritual Bypass Scale (SBS):** Developed by researchers (e.g., Montclair State University) to measure the psychological tendency to use spirituality as an avoidant coping mechanism.
  - **Avoidant Attachment & Dissociation:** Spiritual bypassing functions as a defense mechanism (Dissociation) that mimics equanimity but results in pathological emotional numbness (Apathy). It denies the basic mammalian biological need for bonding and attachment (mediated by Oxytocin).

## 2. Emotional Suppression & Somatization (Stoicism Shadow)
- **Concept:** Conscious inhibition of outward emotional expression (suppression) as a biologically costly regulation strategy that depletes cognitive resources.
- **Key Frameworks:**
  - **Cognitive Load & Executive Depletion:** Emotion regulation via suppression requires active, continuous monitoring and inhibition, which consumes Prefrontal Cortex (PFC) resources and glucose, leading to high cognitive load and reduced executive function.
  - **Autonomic Nervous System (ANS) Dysregulation:** Habitual emotional suppression keeps the sympathetic nervous system ("fight-or-flight") activated, producing chronic cortisol elevation.
  - **Somatization:** Disintegrated processing, where subcortical bodily emotional responses are disconnected from cortical cognitive appraisal, leading the body to express unresolved emotional conflict through physical (somatic) illnesses (muscle tension, chronic fatigue, pain).
  - **Alternative:** Cognitive Reappraisal is a far more resource-efficient and psychologically adaptive strategy.

## 3. Evolutionary Mismatch & Moral Emotions (Christianity Shadow)
- **Concept:** Moral emotions (shame and guilt) evolved to protect social cohesion in ancestral small-group settings, but they create a mismatch in the modern digital world, leading to HPA-axis over-activation.
- **Key Frameworks:**
  - **Social Self-Preservation Theory (Kemeny):** Situations that threaten the "social self" (reputation, status, belonging) trigger shame and activate the HPA axis, releasing Cortisol.
  - **Evolutionary Mismatch:** Constant exposure to global evaluation, digital comparison, and idealized social standards triggers these ancient shame/guilt "smoke detectors" chronically.
  - **Shame-Driven Hiding vs. Guilt-Driven Amending:** Guilt motivatives constructive relationship repair (adaptive), while shame leads to withdrawal, hiding, and self-punishment (maladaptive shame loops).

## 4. Entropy & Self-Organization (Taoism Shadow - Wu Wei Misinterpretation)
- **Concept:** Living organisms are open, dissipative systems that must actively import energy to resist entropy (disorder) and maintain a stable non-equilibrium state.
- **Key Frameworks:**
  - **Dissipative Structures (Ilya Prigogine):** Biological systems far from equilibrium maintain internal order (low entropy) by constantly consuming energy (metabolism) and exporting entropy to the environment.
  - **Second Law of Thermodynamics:** Systems naturally decay toward maximum entropy (disorder/chaos). Passive resignation (stagnation under the guise of "Wu Wei" or letting go) leads to decay, learned helplessness, and death.
  - **Non-Equilibrium Steady States (NESS):** Active maintenance of metabolic fluxes is required to keep systems organized. True "Wu Wei" is not passive inaction; it is friction-free action (flow state), which still requires active energy input.
