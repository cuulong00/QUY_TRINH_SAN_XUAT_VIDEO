# Research Vault — Note 04: Strait of Hormuz Energy Flows & The Macroeconomic Valve

## Metadata & Executive Summary
- **Topic:** Energy Volume Statistics, Geographic Anatomy, and Macroeconomic Sensitivity of the Strait of Hormuz.
- **Source Agencies:** U.S. Energy Information Administration (EIA Country Analysis Briefs), International Energy Agency (IEA), Lloyd’s List Intelligence, TankerTrackers.com.
- **Core Mechanism:** The Critical Valve — How 21 million barrels of petroleum liquids per day passing through a 21-mile-wide strait dictate global marginal energy pricing and sustain global reliance on U.S. naval security architecture.

---

## 1. Geographic & Operational Dimensions
- **Location:** Narrow waterway separating Iran (to the north) and Oman/UAE (to the south), connecting the Persian Gulf with the Gulf of Oman and Arabian Sea.
- **Narrowest Point:** Approximately **21 nautical miles (39 kilometers)** wide.
- **Traffic Separation Scheme (TSS):** Consists of two **2-mile-wide inbound and outbound shipping lanes**, separated by a **2-mile-wide neutral buffer zone**, located within Omani territorial waters.

---

## 2. Verified Volume Data (EIA Energy Transit Analysis)
- **Total Petroleum Transit:**
  - **Volume:** Averages **20.5 to 21.0 million barrels per day (bpd)** of crude oil, condensate, and refined petroleum products.
  - **Global Proportion:** Represents **~21% of total global petroleum liquids consumption** and **nearly 30% of all maritime seaborne crude trade**.
- **Liquefied Natural Gas (LNG):**
  - Approximately **20% of global LNG trade** passes through Hormuz, predominantly exported by Qatar and the UAE to European and Asian power utilities.
- **Destination Markets (The Asymmetry of Exposure):**
  - **Over 82%** of crude oil transiting Hormuz goes directly to Asian markets:
    * **China:** ~3.5–4.0 million bpd.
    * **India:** ~2.5–3.0 million bpd.
    * **Japan:** ~1.8–2.0 million bpd (accounts for ~85%–90% of Japan's total crude consumption).
    * **South Korea:** ~1.3–1.5 million bpd (accounts for ~70% of South Korea's crude consumption).
  - **United States Exposure:** Direct US crude imports from the Persian Gulf have dropped below **0.5 million bpd** (~5% of US imports) due to the domestic shale boom.

---

## 3. The Bypass Pipelines: Hard Physical Limits
Existing overland bypass infrastructure cannot replace the maritime waterway:
1. **Saudi East-West Petroline:** 746 miles from Abqaiq to Yanbu on the Red Sea. Nameplate capacity: **5.0 million bpd** (can be expanded to 7.0 million bpd), but operating at ~3.5 million bpd.
2. **Abu Dhabi Crude Oil Pipeline (ADCOP):** Transits from Habshan to Fujairah outside the Gulf. Nameplate capacity: **1.5 to 1.8 million bpd**.
3. **Total Global Bypass Capacity:** **~3.5 to 4.0 million bpd** of currently unused bypass capacity exists.
4. **The Physical Deficit:** If the Strait of Hormuz is closed or heavily restricted, **at least 16 to 17 million bpd of oil is physically stranded with zero alternative exit route**.

---

## 4. The Macroeconomic & Geoeconomic Connection
- A supply disruption of 10M+ bpd would trigger an immediate spike in crude prices towards **$150–$200/barrel**, creating severe stagflation across import-dependent European and East Asian economies.
- Because oil remains overwhelmingly priced and settled in USD, energy price spikes trigger a violent **short squeeze for U.S. Dollars** as importers worldwide must liquidate other assets to procure dollars for emergency fuel purchases.
- Thus, the U.S. Fifth Fleet stationed in Manama, Bahrain, does not merely protect physical ships; it guarantees the continuity of the petrodollar pricing system itself.
