### [23] The Architecture of Deception: A Historical and Historiographical Deconstruction of the Napoleon Hill Legend

> Concurrently, court and business records show that Hill spent 1908 managing a series of legal crises.[1, 10] In May 1908, Hill was arrested in Mobile, Alabama, on charges of altering checks, though he was eventually acquitted.[10] Throughout 1908, Hill co-managed the Acree-Hill Lumber Company in Mobile, Alabama.[3, 10] Under his direction, the firm purchased thousands of dollars of lumber on credit from national suppliers, sold it immediately for cash below market value to local buyers, and pocketed the cash instead of paying the suppliers.[1, 10] In September 1908—the exact period he claimed to be interviewing Carnegie—Hill fled Alabama to escape warrants for check altering and mail fraud, having left his suppliers with unpaid debts.[2, 10] He arrived in Washington, D.C., where he dropped his first name, "Oliver," and adopted "Napoleon" to evade authorities.[2, 3] Simultaneously, his second marriage, to Edith Whitman, was collapsing.[2, 3] Whitman filed for divorce in 1908, presenting court testimony that characterized Hill as a man of "violent and ungovernable temper" who patronized prostitutes and physically abused both her and their infant daughter.[2, 7]
