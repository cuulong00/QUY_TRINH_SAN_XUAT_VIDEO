He made a deal to serve as the franchising agent for the McDonald brothers. Soon afterwards, he bought them out, and grew the chain into a global empire. Inspiration struck Kabat-Zinn after earning his doctorate in molecular biology at MIT. A dedicated meditator, he had a sudden vision in the midst of a retreat: he could adapt Buddhist teachings and practices to help hospital patients deal with physical pain, stress and anxiety. His masterstroke was the branding of mindfulness as a secular crypto-Buddhist spirituality.

> Furthermore, selective outcome reporting allows researchers to choose which variables to publish based on statistical significance.[31] None of the 21 pre-registered trials in Coronado-Montoya's analysis adequately specified a single primary outcome measure with its time and metric of assessment, leaving massive room for researchers to relegate non-significant primary outcomes to secondary status post-hoc.[36, 38] This is particularly problematic because a Nature paper revealed that due to selective reporting, meta-analyses in psychology produce effect sizes that are on average three times larger than large-scale, pre-registered replications.[31] This "white hat bias"—the distortion of data because the research goal is seen as righteous—is highly prevalent among mindfulness researchers, many of whom are "true believers".[31, 40] This bias leads to the selective reporting of composite scores; for example, researchers will report that mindfulness significantly improved an aggregate composite score of psychological distress for high school adolescents, while failing to highlight that when anxiety or depression were analyzed independently, the therapeutic benefits entirely disappeared.[40] It also obscures the distinction between efficacy (clinical performance under ideal research conditions) and effectiveness (real-world clinical utility).[40] Standard statistical tests of publication bias (such as Egger’s test for funnel plot asymmetry) are rarely conducted (only 29.5% of 44 meta-analyses evaluated publication bias) and are statistically unviable when trials are small or have limited sample size variance, allowing these severe distortions to persist unnoticed.[27, 38]
