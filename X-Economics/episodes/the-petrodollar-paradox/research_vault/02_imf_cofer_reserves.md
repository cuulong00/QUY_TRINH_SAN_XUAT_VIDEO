# Research Vault — Note 02: IMF COFER Reserves, FX Turnover & The Realities of De-Dollarization

## Metadata & Executive Summary
- **Topic:** Empirical Allocation of Global Foreign Exchange Reserves, Currency Turnover Shares, and Central Bank Gold Buying.
- **Source Agencies:** International Monetary Fund (IMF COFER Database), Bank for International Settlements (BIS Triennial Survey), World Gold Council.
- **Core Mechanism:** The Asymmetric Transition — While the USD reserve share is gradually eroding (from >71% in 2000 to ~56.5%–57.8% in 2025/2026), non-traditional currencies and physical gold have absorbed the shift, while the Chinese Renminbi remains locked at ~2.0%–2.7% due to capital controls.

  - Physical gold represents **~15%–18%** of total global central bank reserves, reaching its highest proportion since the mid-1990s as central banks (notably China, Poland, Singapore, Turkey, India) seek sanctions-proof reserve collateral.

- **BIS Foreign Exchange Turnover (Daily Volume: ~$7.5 Trillion):**

  - **Japanese Yen:** 16.7%.
  - **Chinese Renminbi:** 7.0% (mostly paired against USD in onshore/offshore hubs).

## 2. The Structural Mechanics: Why Alternatives Face Hard Ceilings
1. **The Euro's Fiscal Fragmentation:**
   The Eurozone lacks a unified sovereign bond (no Eurobond market comparable to US Treasuries). Buying "Euro debt" means choosing between ultra-safe but negative/low-yielding German Bunds or politically volatile Italian/Spanish sovereign paper.
2. **The Renminbi's Capital Account Dilemma:**
   China cannot offer a deep reserve asset without dismantling its capital account controls. As long as foreign investors face regulatory quotas, repatriation restrictions, and currency peg management, global central banks cannot park trillions of dollars in onshore Chinese assets safely.
3. **The Gold Storage and Settlement Bottleneck:**
   Gold offers zero yield, incurs substantial physical storage/insurance costs, and cannot be used directly to settle instantaneous multi-billion-dollar trade transactions via high-speed electronic ledgers.

## 3. The 2022 Weaponization Shock
- In February 2022, the G7 immobilized approximately **$300 billion** in Russian central bank reserves and disconnected major Russian banks from SWIFT.
- **The Psychological Fracture:** For the first time since World War II, sovereign foreign exchange reserves held in Western custodial accounts were treated as conditional political pledges rather than absolute property rights. This accelerated bilateral local-currency settlements across the Global South.
